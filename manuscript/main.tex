\begin{abstract}
Adaptive network intrusion detection systems retrain their classifier when a drift monitor fires, but a drift alarm only detects change: it does not establish that the retrained challenger should replace the deployed incumbent. We study promotion as a separate decision and isolate two upstream comparability conditions --- how the challenger is constructed and how much evidence it is trained on --- with preregistered controls on three public benchmarks (CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, ToN-IoT). Incumbent-owned frozen preprocessing amplified the apparent harm of promotion; with self-contained challenger pipelines the mean full-drift harm does not persist. Candidate evidence matters in both regimes: at zero drift the mean deficit of 512-per-class challengers disappears at nominal 2{,}000-per-class parity, and under real drift raising nominal candidate evidence from 512 to 2{,}000 per class improves promotion outcomes by $+0.82$, $+1.66$ and $+1.00$ balanced-accuracy points across the three benchmarks (registered outcome: homogeneous size benefit). Validation is conditional: labeled-probe gates help when challengers are evidence-disadvantaged, but under the final size-matched configuration they add no average improvement and strict gating can impose a measurable cost. A registered common-harness comparison finds the published estimator ATC and a calibrated ensemble compatible with always-deploy at full drift while other alternatives pay material costs; no evaluated policy dominates. Establishing incumbent--challenger comparability should therefore precede both promotion and validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}


Machine-learning network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) are trained on historical traffic \cite{nguyen2023tsids}, but the traffic they monitor evolves. New services, shifting user behaviour, reconfigured infrastructure and novel attacks move the input distribution away from the training distribution --- concept drift \cite{shyaa2024evolving,wahab2022intrusion,roshan2018adaptive}. A frozen classifier loses accuracy, so operational NIDS periodically \textbf{retrain} on recent traffic \cite{wahab2022intrusion,camarda2025managing,constantinides2019novel}, and the dominant answer to \emph{when} is \textbf{drift detection}: compare recent traffic with a reference window and retrain when a statistical monitor signals change \cite{gama2004learning,rabanser2019failing,lukats2024benchmark}. A large literature therefore competes to build more sensitive monitors, from univariate change detectors to multivariate two-sample tests \cite{gretton2012kernel,szekely2013energy} and quantum-kernel similarity measures \cite{havlicek2019supervised,schuld2019quantum,kaissar2026enhancing}, on the implicit premise that a better change detector yields a better retraining policy.


This paper separates two questions that the detect-then-retrain loop merges. Drift detection answers \emph{has the distribution changed?} Promotion asks \emph{is this particular challenger sufficiently supported to replace this incumbent?} A drift alarm --- or a schedule, or a false alarm --- \emph{proposes} a challenger; it does not establish that the challenger beats the incumbent. Between the alarm and the promotion decision sits a layer the loop leaves implicit, and this paper makes it explicit: \emph{candidate comparability}. Before a promotion outcome can be interpreted, let alone automated, the evaluation must establish that incumbent and challenger are comparable in how they were \emph{constructed} and in how much \emph{evidence} supports them. Prior work has framed retraining as a cost--benefit decision rather than an automatic response to detected change \cite{zliobaite2015cost,mahadevan2024cost,regol2025retrain}; we add the comparability audit that must precede any such decision, and we show experimentally that it can reverse the conclusions drawn without it.


\textbf{Scope of the net-harm findings.} Several controlled configurations in this paper show always-deploy updating losing to never adapting. These net-harm observations are \emph{configuration-dependent}: they were produced by specific candidate-construction and evidence conditions, and we do not claim them as universal properties of adaptive NIDS or of retraining. The program traces the mean harm to two asymmetries between incumbent and challenger --- a frozen, incumbent-owned preprocessing representation, and a four-fold nominal training-evidence disadvantage --- each isolated by a preregistered control. Configurations without those asymmetries behave differently: robust learners (random forest, logistic regression, MLP) stay non-negative where the fragile SVC-RBF pipeline goes net-negative, and self-contained, size-matched challengers show no mean deficit at zero drift and a larger benefit under drift. Every harm figure below should therefore be read as a statement about the configuration that produced it.


The evidence is staged. An initial controlled study exposed the phenomenon under a historical configuration; preregistered corrective replications then isolated each comparability factor with fresh seeds, frozen margins and machine-evaluable outcome rules; two further registered blocks --- a size-matched control under real drift and a common-harness comparison with published and reference baselines --- complete the design. Throughout, \emph{preregistered} denotes a protocol for an individual stage that was specified and sealed in version control before that stage ran; it does not denote one external registry covering the sequential program as a whole.


Building on active-testing and limited-label model-selection research \cite{sawade2012active,kossen2021active,karimi2021online,han2024model,okanovic2025models}, the paper makes three contributions.

\textbf{C1 (primary --- candidate comparability before promotion): a decision decomposition.} Adaptive updating is decomposed into \emph{drift trigger} $\to$ \emph{candidate construction} $\to$ \emph{evidence comparability} $\to$ \emph{optional validation} $\to$ \emph{promotion} $\to$ \emph{future deployment outcome} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Each stage answers a distinct question: an alarm proposes; construction determines whether the challenger \emph{can} compete under comparable conditions; evidence comparability determines whether the comparison is informative; validation estimates the sign of the specific proposal; and promotion is a decision a healthy incumbent can win.

\textbf{C2 (primary): controlled identification of two comparability asymmetries.} Preprocessing ownership and nominal candidate-evidence size materially change promotion outcomes. The mean full-drift harm does not persist once each challenger owns its preprocessing (a role-randomized experiment localizes the mechanism to feature scaling); the residual zero-drift mean deficit of 512-per-class challengers disappears at nominal 2{,}000-per-class parity; and --- the new registered result --- under real drift raising nominal candidate evidence from 512 to 2{,}000 per class improves promotion outcomes materially in all three benchmarks (\S\ref{sec:symmetric}--\S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift}).

\textbf{C3 (secondary --- conditional validation and context-dependent policies).} Point/strict validation materially helps when the challenger's construction or evidence is asymmetric and provides no detectable average benefit --- with a measurable strict-gate cost in one cell --- once challengers are self-contained and evidence-matched, at zero drift and under drift alike (\S\ref{sec:gatevalue}). A registered common-harness comparison with published and reference alternatives shows that validation and its alternatives have context-dependent value rather than a globally dominant policy, and that even the apparent ordering of update policies depends on the comparability of the candidate generator being evaluated (\S\ref{sec:baselines}).

Supporting instruments and boundaries (tertiary --- formal, diagnostic and operational instruments) complete the study: the sequential VBC-SG family is a secondary formal instrument for probe-level false-superiority control; a quantum-kernel monitor serves only as a detector-invariance check; thirteen chronological replays delimit external empirical support without estimating deployment frequency; and an acquisition-yield simulation bounds attack-label discovery cost (\S\ref{sec:boundaries}). These are not universal policies or primary contributions. The paper proceeds: related work (\S2), the decision pipeline and gates (\S3), experimental design (\S4), results (\S5), implications and limits (\S6--\S8).


\section{Related work}


\textbf{Concept drift and adaptive intrusion detection.} Concept drift degrades ML-based NIDS, and a body of work adapts models online or on a schedule to compensate \cite{gama2014survey,lu2019learning,shyaa2024evolving}. Most such systems assume adaptation is beneficial and focus on \emph{how} to adapt (incremental learning, windowing, ensembles) \cite{constantinides2019novel,roshan2018adaptive,shyaa2023enhanced}. We instead study \emph{whether and when} promoting a retrained candidate helps, and show that the answer can reverse with the way the candidate is built and evidenced --- connecting to, but distinct from, work on negative transfer and catastrophic forgetting in continual learning \cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic,kirkpatrick2017overcoming,zhang2023survey}.


\textbf{Drift detection and two-sample monitors.} Drift is commonly detected with change detectors \cite{bifet2007learning,gama2004learning,baena2006early,raab2020reactive}, statistical-test ensembles over batch streams \cite{komorniczak2022ensemble}, or multivariate two-sample tests --- MMD, energy distance, Kolmogorov--Smirnov and Jensen--Shannon divergence --- used as retraining or alarm triggers \cite{gretton2012kernel,rizzo2016energy,lin1991divergence,darling1957ks}; how drift manifests (type, locality, severity) itself varies widely across streams \cite{aguiar2024locality}. Our classical baselines are drawn from this family. A complementary family of \emph{performance-aware} detectors monitors the model's error signal directly, presupposing continuous labeled feedback; Bayram et al. \cite{bayram2022degradation} survey them and formalize the distinction between distributional change and model degradation that our degradation--headroom analysis (Supplementary \S S1.1) quantifies empirically. We use distribution-level monitors and find that improving them did not improve the promotion decision in any regime we evaluated, because they are blind to model degradation; the reference DDM and ADWIN implementations are evaluated as retraining triggers in the registered common-harness comparison (\S\ref{sec:baselines}).


\textbf{Quantum kernels for drift monitoring.} Quantum-kernel methods embed data via parameterized quantum feature maps and have been proposed for classification and similarity estimation \cite{havlicek2019supervised,schuld2019quantum,huang2021power}, including exploratory use in cybersecurity \cite{gouveia2020towards,nicesio2023quantum,bellante2025evaluating}. Evidence that fidelity-based quantum kernels can improve out-of-distribution robustness under shift --- the operative property being the kernel's geometry rather than its quantumness --- motivated evaluating quantum-kernel MMD as a drift monitor here. For the \emph{promotion decision}, changing the detector family --- quantum or classical --- did not change the outcome in any regime we evaluated, consistent with evidence that quantum-kernel advantage claims require careful classical baselining and face concentration limits \cite{schnabel2025quantum,thanasilp2024concentration}.


\textbf{Drift, rejection and evaluation discipline in ML security.} A parallel security literature asks when a deployed classifier should no longer be trusted. Transcend \cite{jordaney2017transcend} and its revision Transcendent \cite{barbero2022transcendent} use conformal evaluation to reject individual predictions whose low credibility signals model aging; CADE \cite{yang2021cade} detects and explains drifting samples via contrastive representations; INSOMNIA \cite{andresini2021insomnia} updates a NIDS continuously with semi-supervision as drift accrues. These systems abstain on individual samples or adapt continuously; none evaluates whether a specific retrained candidate should replace the incumbent, and none can be instantiated in this decision problem without changing what it decides (\S\ref{sec:baselines}). TESSERACT \cite{pendlebury2019tesseract} and the ``dos and don'ts'' of Arp et al. \cite{arp2022dodonts} document how temporal and experimental bias inflate security-ML results; our pre-specified protocol with temporally ordered progressive windows and paired within-run baselines (\S4) follows that discipline.


\textbf{When to retrain: cost-aware and validation-gated updates.} Beyond detection, some work weighs the cost of adaptation or validates a candidate before deployment, e.g. in MLOps model-update pipelines and shadow/champion--challenger evaluation \cite{nath2007champion,sculley2015hidden,kreuzberger2023mlops,guissouma2023continuous}; that a well-intentioned update can be net-harmful is documented beyond security \cite{bansal2019updates}. Champion--challenger evaluation, validation gates and label-efficient model comparison are therefore not new, and this paper claims none of them as novel. Their reliance on a small labeled probe connects to active and label-efficient learning for data streams \cite{cacciarelli2024active,krawczyk2019adaptive,liu2021comprehensive}. A complementary line estimates a model's accuracy under distribution shift without target labels \cite{garg2022atc,guillory2021doc,deng2021labels,baek2022agreement,jiang2022disagreement,rosenfeld2023dis2}; rather than argue past it, we instantiate its two standard estimators (ATC, DoC) as promotion rules under the same harness as our gates (\S\ref{sec:baselines}).

\textbf{Positioning and novelty.} Table~\ref{tab:positioning} positions the closest decision formulations by objective, decision unit, required information, output and guarantee. The rows are complementary rather than an empirical ranking: their problem definitions, information sets and outputs differ, and this paper reports no head-to-head superiority claim against them. What this study adds is not a new gate but an experimentally supported \emph{decomposition} of adaptive promotion: existing adaptive-NIDS work generally focuses on detecting drift or on adapting models \cite{shyaa2024evolving,lu2019learning,constantinides2019novel}, whereas we separate the promotion decision from the alarm and demonstrate, with preregistered controls, that upstream challenger construction and evidence conditions can reverse both the apparent harm of promotion and the apparent relative value of update policies. Comparability of candidates is a familiar concern in model comparison generally \cite{sawade2012active,karimi2021online}; we make no claim that it has never been considered, only that its consequences for drift-triggered promotion had not been isolated experimentally under the controls used here.

\section{Candidate promotion as a decision pipeline}


\subsection{Promotion as a sequential decision}


We consider a deployed binary classifier $h_0$ (benign vs. attack) monitoring a stream of traffic windows $W_1, W_2, \dots$ Each window $W_t$ is a set of flows with (at evaluation time) ground-truth labels; the deployed model degrades as the traffic distribution drifts. A drift monitor $D$ produces a score $s_t = D(W_t)$ measuring divergence of $W_t$ from a reference. The standard adaptive-NIDS loop is: raise an alarm when $s_t$ exceeds a threshold, and upon a confirmed alarm \textbf{retrain} the classifier on recent data. We make explicit that this loop conflates two questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{detection:} \emph{has the distribution changed?} --- answered by $D$;
  \item \textbf{decision:} \emph{will replacing $h$ with a model retrained on the current window improve accuracy?} --- which $D$ does not answer.
\end{itemize}


Let $h$ be the incumbent model at proposal time $t$ and $h'$ a candidate retrained on the current regime. For a deployment horizon $H$, define the candidate's realized future value relative to retaining the incumbent as
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_t(h',h;H)
  &=\sum_{k=1}^{H} w_k\!\left[\mathrm{BA}(h',W_{t+k})-\mathrm{BA}(h,W_{t+k})\right],\\
w_k&\geq0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{H}w_k=1 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Promotion is beneficial over that horizon iff $V_t(h',h;H)>0$ (or exceeds a deployment-cost equivalent expressed in the same units). This is a future, generally unobserved quantity at decision time. The labeled probe instead supplies a contemporaneous estimate of the sign of the candidate--incumbent difference and is evaluated here as a surrogate promotion rule; it is not an observation of $V_t$. Because $D$ measures distributional change rather than the sign of this relative value, a detector-only policy commits every confirmed change, including changes for which retraining does not help. The full pipeline this paper decomposes and evaluates (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}) distinguishes the distributional alarm, the candidate proposal, the evidence used to \emph{train} the candidate, the evidence used to \emph{validate} it, and the evidence that determines its \emph{future deployment} value --- five different objects that the detection-centric loop treats as one. The gates of \S\ref{sec:gates} instantiate the validation stage; the controls of \S\ref{sec:construction} instantiate the construction and comparability stages.

\subsection{Drift monitors}


We use two-sample drift monitors comparing a reference window (from the pre-drift regime) to the current window. Classical baselines: \textbf{Energy distance}, \textbf{RBF-MMD}, per-feature \textbf{Kolmogorov--Smirnov} (max reduction) and histogram \textbf{Jensen--Shannon} divergence. Quantum monitors: \textbf{quantum-kernel MMD} with \textbf{ZZ} and \textbf{Pauli-XZ} feature maps ($q_{\text{reps}}=1$, angle encoding with standardized \texttt{atan} scaling), the MMD being computed in the quantum-kernel-induced RKHS. All monitors expose a scalar score; thresholds are calibrated per detector on held-out calibration windows at a fixed quantile (0.95). The paper's claims are detector-agnostic; the quantum monitors are instruments testing whether a more expressive kernel changes the decision (it does not; Supplementary \S S1.2).


\subsection{Progressive-drift readaptation protocol}


Each stream has 100 post-drift windows. Window $t$ is a class-balanced sample in which each class is a mixture of the reference and current pools with mixing weight $\mathrm{sev}(t)$ ("severity"), ramped linearly from 0 to 1 over the first 80 windows and held at 1 thereafter, so drift arrives gradually. On a committed adaptation, the candidate is trained on a fresh balanced labeled sample from the current regime, and the detector reference is reset to the adapted regime. The trigger policy (alarm confirmation and cooldown) is held fixed across all gate conditions, so the decision gate is the only \emph{designed} difference between arms. On the confirmatory harness (v2) every arm additionally processes \emph{bit-identical} streams, so contrasts are exactly paired; only the initial exploratory study drew a separate pseudo-random realization per arm (identical in distribution, not in realization; \S4).


\subsection{The validate-before-commit gate}
\label{sec:gates}


The gate is a decision step interposed between the trigger and the model swap: a \emph{deployment} (commit) gate, not a retraining gate. On each confirmed trigger the loop retrains a candidate $h'$ as usual --- consuming its training labels and compute regardless of the outcome --- and the gate then decides only whether $h'$ replaces the incumbent $h$; otherwise we keep $h$ (and keep the detector reference, so a later window can re-propose once an update becomes worthwhile). \S\ref{sec:v2} additionally evaluates a two-stage variant that moves the same probe \emph{before} candidate training, gating the training decision itself. We evaluate three commit gates:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{none} (naive):} always commit --- the standard loop.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{labeled\_probe}$(b,\varepsilon)$:} draw a small balanced labeled probe $P$ of $b$ flows from the current window; commit iff $\mathrm{BA}(h',P) \ge \mathrm{BA}(h,P) + \varepsilon$. The probe is the method's incremental labeling cost; we report $b\in\{32,64\}$, $\varepsilon=0$.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{unsup\_disagree}$(\tau)$:} commit iff the fraction of flows on which $h$ and $h'$ disagree on the \emph{unlabeled} current window is $\ge \tau$ (a zero-label proxy for "adaptation would change decisions"); we report $\tau=0.15$.
\end{itemize}

We evaluate and state both variants, which differ in exactly the information they are allowed to use. Algorithm 1A is the \emph{core simulation} gate: the environment supplies candidate batches and probes from its class pools at the current mixture severity --- convenient, and the source of the paper's main tables, but an oracle a deployment lacks. Algorithm 1B is the \emph{observed-data} gate: every object it touches --- the candidate's training rows, the probe, and even the detector's post-commit reference --- comes from windows the system has actually seen, and nothing reads $\mathrm{sev}(t)$. \S\ref{sec:v2} reports 1B as a registered replication of 1A.

\begin{verbatim}
Algorithm 1A: Gated update, core simulation harness (one stream)
  h <- h0 ; D <- fit_reference(pools, sev=0) ; alarms <- [] ; cooldown <- 0
  for t = 1..T:
      W_t <- environment.stream[t]              # pre-generated, balanced, labeled
      record BA(h, W_t)
      s_t <- D.score(W_t) ; alarms.append(s_t > threshold)
      trigger <- policy(alarms) and cooldown == 0
      if trigger:
          h' <- train(pools.train, sev(t))      # candidate: current mixture (oracle)
          P   <- sample(pools.probe, sev(t))    # probe: balanced, b flows (oracle)
          commit <- [ BA(h',P) >= BA(h,P) + eps ]                  # Sec 3.4
          if commit:
              h <- h' ; D <- fit_reference(pools, sev(t))          # deploy + reset
          alarms <- [] ; cooldown <- C                             # applied either way
      else: cooldown <- max(0, cooldown - 1)
  return {BA(h, W_t)}_t

Algorithm 1B: Gated update, observed-data harness (no sev(t), no pools)
  ... identical control flow, but at a trigger at window t:
      h' <- train(rows of the last 8 OBSERVED windows)             # candidate
      P  <- 32 rows of the OBSERVED window t-9, natural composition,
            excluding by row identity any row used to train h'     # probe
      commit <- [ acc(h',P) >= acc(h,P) + eps ]
      if commit: h <- h' ; D <- fit_reference(last 8 OBSERVED windows),
                 threshold <- quantile_0.95(scores of preceding OBSERVED windows)
\end{verbatim}


The \texttt{labeled\_probe} gate estimates the contemporaneous sign of the candidate--incumbent difference from $b$ labels, the surrogate for future value identified in \S3.1; the \texttt{unsup\_disagree} gate tests whether that estimate can be obtained without labels.


\textbf{A cost-sensitive reading: two decisions, two thresholds.} The update loop makes two sequential decisions with different cost structures. Let each error cost $\lambda_e$ per flow, deploying an update $\lambda_U$, training a candidate $\lambda_C$ (compute plus training labels), and probe labels $\lambda_L$ each. \emph{At commit time the candidate already exists}: $\lambda_C$ and the probe are sunk, so over a horizon of $N$ post-decision flows the myopic commit rule is
$$\mathrm{commit} \iff \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}(h',P) - \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}(h,P) \ge \varepsilon^{\ast}, \qquad \varepsilon^{\ast} = \frac{\lambda_U}{\lambda_e N},$$
--- the margin $\varepsilon$ is the \emph{deployment} cost expressed in accuracy points over the remaining horizon, and $\lambda_C$ does not belong in it, because rejecting the candidate cannot recover it. Training costs enter one decision earlier --- whether to build a candidate at all, which pays off only if the \emph{expected} committed gain exceeds $(\lambda_C + \lambda_U)/(\lambda_e N)$; the two-stage gate of \S\ref{sec:v2} instantiates exactly this structure, spending the probe on the incumbent \emph{before} training. A risk-averse operator replaces the point estimate with a lower confidence bound (the LCB variant of \S\ref{sec:v2}); raising $\varepsilon$ from 0 to 0.01--0.02 cuts committed updates by ${\sim}30\%$ at equal downstream gain (Supplementary \S S1.5), and asymmetric error costs substitute a weighted accuracy for BA in the same rule.


\subsection{Risk-controlled commit gates and the scope of their guarantee}
\label{sec:riskgates}

This subsection states the risk-controlled alternatives to the point gate at the level needed to read the results. It is deliberately minimal: implementation details, the deferral continuation modes, the spending schedules and the full list of stated properties are relocated to Supplementary \S S2.13, and the proof of Proposition~1 to \S S4. VBC-SG is a secondary decision-support instrument for settings in which validation is chosen; it is not the source of the paper's main conclusion, and we claim neither that any member of the family dominates nor mathematical novelty for the construction.

\textbf{(A) Point and strict validation.} The point gate ($\varepsilon{=}0$) commits on any observed probe advantage. It maximizes the probe point estimate, not future accuracy, and carries no formal error control: about a quarter of its commits have negative realized value (\S\ref{sec:v2}). The \emph{strict} variant additionally rejects probe ties, at no additional labels; \S\ref{sec:risk} shows this no-additional-label rule is the better buy at some operating points.

\textbf{(B) Risk-controlled validation.} Let the probe be $b$ flows drawn from a distribution $\mathcal P$, and for each flow let
$$d_i = \mathbf 1[h'\text{ correct on }i] - \mathbf 1[h\text{ correct on }i]\in\{-1,0,+1\}.$$
Three rules commit only on statistical evidence that $\mathbb E_{\mathcal P}[d]>0$: (i) \textbf{exact McNemar} \cite{mcnemar1947note} --- a one-sided exact test on the discordant pairs at level $\alpha$ (ties never commit); (ii) \textbf{Robbins CS} --- a normal-mixture (sub-Gaussian, $\sigma^2\le1$) lower confidence sequence \cite{howard2021timeuniform}, committing at the first look whose lower bound exceeds $0$; (iii) \textbf{empirical-Bernstein CS} --- a predictable-plug-in betting confidence sequence \cite{waudbysmith2023betting} using the empirical variance of $d_i$, far tighter because the $d_i$ are mostly ties. The CS gates inspect in 16-flow blocks up to the budget $b$ and never commit if the lower bound never clears $0$: an undecided proposal is rejected. The confidence sequences run at $\alpha=0.10$ and the exact-McNemar gate at its registered $\alpha=0.05$; re-running McNemar at $\alpha=0.10$ leaves the zero-drift conclusion unchanged ($\approx 0$ commits; \S\ref{sec:risk}), and the effective per-arm $\alpha$ is recorded in the run manifest.

\textbf{(C) Pooled versus stratified guarantees.} The probe is drawn with \emph{fixed class quotas} ($b/2$ per class), not i.i.d.\ from one distribution, so the pooled tests above match the sampling only approximately. \emph{Stratified} variants lower-bound each class's mean correctness difference at $\alpha/2$ (Bonferroni over the two classes) and commit iff $\tfrac12(L_{\mathrm{ben}}+L_{\mathrm{att}})>0$, which bounds the false-superiority probability for the \emph{balanced-accuracy} difference under the design actually used. Only the anytime-valid stratified version, in which each class's bound is an empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence, supports the anytime-valid claim; a fixed-sample per-class LCB variant has no sequential validity.

\textbf{(D) VBC-SG: commit, reject or defer.} The \emph{Validate-Before-Commit Sequential Gate} is the member that carries every layer of control at once. Per-class empirical-Bernstein sequences drive a three-action decision: \textsc{commit} when the balanced-accuracy lower bound clears zero, \textsc{reject} when the upper bound falls below zero (futility), and \textsc{defer} otherwise --- the candidate is retained and evidence continues at later windows, up to a deferral cap $D$, rejecting at the cap. Because the stream can drift between windows, a deferred sequence must state what it estimates; three registered continuation modes do so (\emph{Cohort-sim}, \emph{Refresh} and \emph{Accumulate}; Supplementary \S S2.13). Cohort-sim resamples the proposal-time target in the simulator and is not a retained production cohort, so that acquisition scheme is not claimed to be directly deployable. A deployment-long risk budget allocates each proposal's level $\alpha_j$ by a declared spending schedule (Bonferroni or $p$-series), and the effective $\alpha_j$ of every proposal is logged.

\textbf{(E) What VBC-SG guarantees.} For a single proposal and \emph{under the probe distribution} $\mathcal P$, the confidence-sequence gates satisfy
$$\Pr\!\big(\exists n\le b:\ \text{LCB}_n>0 \ \big|\ \mathbb E_{\mathcal P}[d]\le 0\big)\ \le\ \alpha ,$$
uniformly over the data-dependent stopping time: they control the probability of \emph{falsely establishing positive expected paired correctness on the probe distribution}. Under the spending schedule, the union bound gives a deployment-long version: the probability of \emph{ever} making such a false commit over the deployment is at most $\alpha_{\mathrm{life}}$, under the same sampling assumptions. Without a schedule the guarantee is per proposal only. Labels per proposal are bounded by the per-window budget times $(1+D)$, and the observed cost concentrates well below that bound. For the accumulate mode the per-proposal bound holds under a weaker condition:

\textbf{Proposition 1 (conditional validity of the commit e-process).} Map each per-flow correctness difference to $x_i = (d_i+1)/2 \in [0,1]$; the empirical-Bernstein sequence implemented in the artifact is a wealth process $W_n$ with predictable betting fractions and plug-in means, whose commit condition $\mathrm{LCB}_n > 0$ is algebraically the event $W_n > 1/\alpha$. If $\mathbb{E}[d_i \mid \mathcal F_{i-1}] \le 0$ for every $i$ --- the weak null; no stationarity or identical distribution is assumed --- then $W_n$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with $W_0 = 1$, and Ville's inequality gives $\Pr(\exists n : W_n > 1/\alpha) \le \alpha$. The proof (Supplementary \S S4) is the standard testing-by-betting argument \cite{waudbysmith2023betting,howard2021timeuniform}; we claim no novelty, only the correspondence to this implementation, which a registered null simulation with time-varying conditional means and variances verifies empirically.

\textbf{(F) What VBC-SG does not guarantee.} None of the above bounds the probability that a committed candidate lowers \emph{future} balanced accuracy. The probe-level bound and a future-deployment bound coincide only when the probe is representative of the windows the candidate will be evaluated on, and the paper documents where that premise fails --- the stale-probe and dependence results of \S\ref{sec:boundaries}. We therefore claim a bound on \emph{false probe-superiority}, never on future deployment regret or harm; \S\ref{sec:risk} reports the empirical per-stream harmful-commit accounting and the label price of each layer.


\section{Experimental design}


\subsection{Datasets and controlled streams}


\textbf{Datasets and regimes.} CICIDS2017 \cite{sharafaldin2018toward} (Tuesday reference $\to$ Wednesday, Friday-DDoS, Friday-PortScan, Thursday-WebAttacks), UNSW-NB15 \cite{moustafa2015unsw} (DoS, Reconnaissance) and ToN-IoT \cite{alsaedi2020toniot} (Scanning), spanning benefit, marginal/mixed and harmful readaptation. Features are numeric, standardized and PCA-reduced to 8 dimensions; windows contain 128 flows. To rule out label leakage, identifier and label-derived columns are removed at staging (UNSW: \texttt{id}, binary \texttt{label}, \texttt{attack\_cat}; ToN-IoT: \texttt{label}, \texttt{type}, timestamps and IP/uid identifiers; the CICIDS variant used ships no flow identifiers), and we verified that no remaining single feature separates the classes beyond AUC $\approx$ 0.81 --- far from the near-perfect separation a leaked label would produce. CICIDS2017 has documented traffic-generation and labeling artifacts \cite{engelen2021troubleshooting}; we therefore treat it as the benefit end of a three-benchmark spectrum rather than as sole evidence --- the paper's central negative results (the marginal and harmful regimes) come from the independent UNSW-NB15 and ToN-IoT benchmarks.


\textbf{The confirmatory harness --- the methodology behind every headline claim.} All confirmatory results come from a harness built to make the paired contrasts exact. (i) \emph{Common streams:} every evaluation stream and calibration draw is pre-generated by an environment generator that depends on the seed alone, before any policy runs --- all arms process bit-identical windows in the same order, so gate$-$naive contrasts are truly paired and policy actions cannot perturb the environment. (ii) \emph{Role-disjoint partitions:} each per-class pool is split once per seed (50/30/20) into window/train/probe partitions; evaluation windows draw only from the first, initial models, candidates and detector references only from the second, probes only from the third --- train/probe/evaluation overlap is impossible by construction. (iii) \emph{Separate generators:} environment, initial training, detector calibration, candidate training, probe draws and post-commit recalibration each use their own RNG, seeded by (seed) or (seed, $t$) only, never advanced by policy actions. (iv) \emph{Per-trigger logging:} at every trigger the harness records pre-trigger incumbent degradation, the detector score, and incumbent-vs-candidate BA over the following 1/3/5/10 windows --- lookahead used for logging only, never by any policy. The replication protocol (criteria, margins, one-pass stopping rule) was committed and publicly tagged (\texttt{harness-v2-protocol}) before any confirmatory seed ran, with pre-run registered amendments covering seed hygiene, deduplication, baselines and analysis specifications; the confirmatory window is 30 held-out seeds (104--133), untouched by any smoke test.


\textbf{Shared protocol constants.} The initial model trains on 2{,}000 flows per class from the reference regime; in the historical configuration the scaler and PCA are fit on that training set only and frozen thereafter, and every candidate trains on a balanced sample of 512 flows per class drawn \emph{at the current mixture severity} $\mathrm{sev}(t)$ --- never from the final regime pool --- so the candidate sees the population the deployed model faces at the trigger, not the future (a simulator convenience a production system lacks; \S7 bounds the word ``deployable'' accordingly, and the sliding-window and chronological-stream experiments provide the observed-data counterpoints). The comparability controls of \S\ref{sec:construction} vary exactly these two conventions. Detector references use 256 flows per class; thresholds are the 0.95 quantile of detector scores over 30 pre-drift calibration windows; the trigger is 3 consecutive alarms with a 10-window cooldown throughout; windows contain 128 flows. The probe is the gate's \emph{incremental} label cost on top of the labeled retraining data that any labeled-retraining loop --- gated or not --- already consumes: we claim label efficiency of the \emph{commit decision}, not of the retraining pipeline (\S\ref{sec:v2} accounts for both).


\textbf{Label and information latency: exact semantics.} Several arms model the delay between a flow being observed and its label being available, and Table~\ref{tab:latency} fixes them, read directly off the artifact. The probe knob ($L_p$) draws the probe from the stream as of window $t-L_p$; the candidate knob ($L_c$) shifts the candidate's \emph{whole} training batch --- features and labels together, as one adjudicated block: under the pool generator the batch is sampled at the mixture of window $t-L_c$, and under the sliding-window generator it is the eight observed windows ending at $t-L_c$. The observed-data probe is inherently nine windows old. Training completion is instantaneous in the confirmatory, observed-data and chronological arms; the pool-based operational sensitivity (\S\ref{sec:v2}) explicitly evaluates a five-window deployment delay, during which the incumbent keeps serving. While a deferred VBC-SG proposal is pending, the incumbent remains deployed and \emph{new proposals are blocked} --- a fresh alarm during a pending decision is absorbed exactly like a cooldown window, so a proposal is never silently replaced.

\subsection{Candidate construction and evidence controls}
\label{sec:construction}

We evaluate how preprocessing ownership and candidate evidence size alter promotion outcomes through staged, preregistered controls: each factor was isolated by a replication whose protocol, estimands, margins and interpretation rules were frozen before any of its confirmatory seeds ran. The controls were designed sequentially --- each registered after the previous one's outcome was known --- and each is preregistered relative to its own seeds; we present them as the two levels of a construction factor and two drift regimes of an evidence factor rather than as a chronology.

\subsubsection{Transformer ownership: self-contained candidate pipelines}
\label{sec:symmetric_method}

The historical configuration of this program's harness fits scaler and PCA once, on the initial training set, and \emph{freezes} them --- every later challenger is trained and scored inside that initial representation (\texttt{frozen\_initial\_transformer}). The role-randomized control of \S\ref{sec:risk} shows this policy is not neutral for SVC-RBF, so the decisive question is dynamic: does the harmful-update result survive when each challenger is a \emph{self-contained pipeline}? A preregistered replication answers it; protocol, estimands, margins and interpretation rules were frozen before any confirmatory seed ran (in the artifact). Throughout, ``historical'' denotes this program's own earlier harness: we do not claim that frozen incumbent-owned preprocessing is a standard or widespread policy in published adaptive NIDS. The lesson is about asymmetric construction in \emph{evaluation} --- a promotion conclusion can be distorted when incumbent and challenger are compared under asymmetric construction --- not about the prevalence of the policy itself.

\textbf{Design.} The environment emits a \emph{raw} stream: per seed, the same windows are served to every arm, hash-verified bit-identical, with no global transformation. Each model is a \texttt{ModelPipeline} (scaler + PCA + classifier + provenance metadata) that receives raw flows and transforms internally; the drift monitor is a separate \texttt{DetectorPipeline} whose representation stays the initial transformer under \emph{both} policies, so candidate-construction policy is never confounded with monitoring policy. The two transformer policies differ in exactly one intervention: \texttt{frozen\_initial\_transformer} reuses the initial scaler/PCA for every candidate (reproducing the historical harness bit-for-bit against the published v1.20.2 outputs), while \texttt{own\_transformer\_per\_model} fits each challenger's scaler on its own candidate batch only and its PCA on the scaled batch only --- same dimension, solver and classifier hyperparameters; raw candidate batches are bit-identical across policies, the probe is served raw to both pipelines, and neither probe nor future windows enter any fit. A commit deploys the \emph{complete bundle}, serving from window $t{+}1$; scaler/PCA parameter hashes and the SVC's fitted effective $\gamma$ are recorded per candidate.

\textbf{Estimands and inference.} Fresh confirmatory seeds 3001--3030 (never used before; smoke and parity used disjoint seeds), six scenarios (PortScan, UNSW-Recon, ToN-IoT $\times$ full and zero drift), KS-max only, policies never/naive/point/strict. The registered estimands are $\Delta_{\text{naive,own}} = \mathrm{BA}_{\text{naive,own}} - \mathrm{BA}_{\text{never}}$ (does harm persist?), $\Delta_{\text{transformer}} = \mathrm{BA}_{\text{naive,own}} - \mathrm{BA}_{\text{naive,frozen}}$ (the ownership interaction --- the estimand that isolates the ownership effect directly), and $\Delta_{\text{point--naive,own}}$, $\Delta_{\text{strict--naive,own}}$ (does validation retain value?). Four frozen families (harm under own/full; transformer interaction/full; gate value under own/full; a 12-contrast zero-drift secondary family) are tested by the same deterministic centered paired bootstrap as the rest of the paper, Holm-corrected within family; the inferential unit is the \emph{seed} (trajectory means paired through the shared raw stream) --- windows, triggers and commits are never treated as independent units. Margins were frozen in advance: $\pm0.5$ BA points for equivalence, assessed by inclusion of the CI90 within the margin (the interval-inclusion form of the two one-sided tests \cite{schuirmann1987comparison}; $\pm0.2/\pm1.0$ sensitivities), and non-inferiority guardrails of $-1.0$ recall points and $+0.5$ FPR points (one-sided 95\% bounds) that restrict safety \emph{language} --- balanced accuracy alone determines the registered A/B/C interpretation. The $\pm0.5$-point equivalence margin equals the materiality threshold this program preregistered for its own harm claims --- effects below half a balanced-accuracy point are treated as operationally immaterial throughout, and the smallest harm the paper calls material ($-0.65$) exceeds it --- so CI90 inclusion records compatibility of the mean effect with that registered margin; the conclusion remains margin-dependent and does not establish absence of an effect; the $\pm0.2$/$\pm1.0$ sensitivities bracket each verdict's margin-dependence.

\subsubsection{Candidate evidence size at zero drift: the size-matched control}
\label{sec:sizematched_method}

The replication above trains every challenger on 512 flows per class while the incumbent was trained on 2{,}000 per class --- the historical configuration, inherited by every arm of the program to that point. Its residual zero-drift harm therefore admits one last mundane explanation: the challenger simply sees a quarter of the incumbent's evidence. A preregistered control isolates that variable: does harmful candidate promotion under zero drift persist when self-contained challengers are trained with the \emph{same} per-class sample size as the initial incumbent? Protocol, config, seeds, margins, families and machine-evaluable outcome rules were committed before any confirmatory seed ran (in the artifact).

\textbf{Design.} Zero drift only (where the residual harm lives), with the identical v1.21.0 scenario definitions: random proposal trigger ($p{=}0.05$, drift-independent), severity fixed at zero, \texttt{own\_transformer\_per\_model} exclusively, KS-max machinery, same windows, duration, evaluation and statistics. Per benchmark, seven arms --- never-adapt, and \{naive, point, strict\} $\times$ candidate size $\in \{512, 2000\}$/class --- give a 21-arm matrix on 30 fresh confirmatory seeds (4001--4030, verified virgin against every prior ledger). The only new variable is the challenger's training size; trigger, probe (32 labels), gate margins, hyperparameters (SVC $C{=}1.0$, $\gamma{=}$\texttt{scale}, PCA dim 8), temporal semantics and sampling policy are unchanged, and the incumbent keeps its 2{,}000/class training set. One structural property should be stated in advance: at severity zero the 2{,}000-per-class own-pipeline challenger is drawn from the same training pools, by the same balanced sampler and with the same hyperparameters as the incumbent, so it is close to an exchangeable re-draw of the incumbent's own training procedure. Near-zero mean effects at nominal parity are therefore the outcome \emph{expected} of a correct implementation; the control's informative content lies in the direct 512-versus-2{,}000 contrast and in checking that no unanticipated asymmetry remains --- which is exactly why the same intervention is repeated under real drift in \S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift_method}, where the challenger is no longer an exchangeable copy of the incumbent.

\textbf{Nested candidate batches.} The two size conditions are not independently sampled challengers: at every proposal the canonical draw first produces the \emph{unchanged} historical 512-per-class batch (bit-identical to the v1.21.0 arms --- verified by a bit-level parity run), then extends it with 1{,}488 further draws per class from the same per-trigger RNG stream, so the 512 batch \emph{is} the first 512 rows per class of the 2{,}000 batch (same rows, labels, order, hash; 999 proposal-level pairs verified at confirmatory scale). These pool draws are with replacement; the extension therefore adds nominal draws, not necessarily 1{,}488 distinct flows or an equal amount of effective information. The nested size contrast changes nominal training-set size for an otherwise coupled candidate rather than comparing independently sampled challengers. Each challenger's scaler/PCA/classifier still fit on its own batch only, with full per-candidate provenance recorded. This is a controlled pool-based construction --- not an observed-data or deployment-realistic arm --- and what it matches is \emph{nominal} per-class sample size; \S\ref{sec:sizematched} states what nominal parity does and does not equate.

\textbf{Estimands, families and outcome rules (all frozen).} Primary estimands, in BA points per benchmark: E1 size-matched damage ($\mathrm{naive}_{2000}-\mathrm{never}$), E2 candidate-size effect ($\mathrm{naive}_{2000}-\mathrm{naive}_{512}$), E3/E4 gate value at matched size, E5 (secondary) the gate$\times$size interaction. Four Holm-corrected families (F1/F2/F3/F4: 3/3/6/6 contrasts), the same deterministic centered paired bootstrap, CI95 for harm/superiority, CI90 for equivalence at the reused $\pm0.5$-point margin, recall/FPR guardrails as before; the inferential unit remains the seed. Three machine-evaluable outcomes were frozen in advance --- PERSISTENCE (material Holm-significant damage at 2{,}000 plus a guardrail-clean gate win), ELIMINATION (equivalence of all three damage contrasts \emph{and} all six gate contrasts, plus no contradicting harmful-future-value signal), ATTENUATION (anything else) --- with the harmful-future-value criterion keyed, by preregistration, to the \emph{sign rate} of committed proposals' five-window future value. \S\ref{sec:sizematched} applies these rules literally.

\subsubsection{Candidate evidence size under real drift: the registered full-drift control}
\label{sec:sizematched_drift_method}

The zero-drift control cannot, by construction, show that candidate evidence matters where recency carries genuinely shifted information. A registered control therefore repeats the nested size intervention under full progressive drift --- the regime in which the historical frozen-transformer harness had reported that size-matching \emph{deepened} harm (Supplementary \S S1.5). Its protocol, outcome rules and analysis script were frozen and committed before any implementation of the mechanism and before any smoke or confirmatory seed; the implementation was then validated against the sealed outputs (bit-identical byte parity of the flag-off and nested-512 paths with stored smoke outputs) before the confirmatory block ran.

\textbf{Design.} Three full-drift scenarios (PortScan, UNSW-Recon, ToN-IoT; mixing ramp 0$\to$1 over 80 of 100 windows), seven arms each --- never-adapt and \{naive, point, strict\} $\times$ candidate size $\in\{512, 2000\}$/class --- on 30 fresh confirmatory seeds (6001--6030, verified virgin), \texttt{own\_transformer\_per\_model} exclusively, every other constant byte-identical to the symmetric replication. \emph{Nested draw at drift:} at a proposal at window $t$ the canonical draw first produces the historical 512-per-class batch at the proposal-time severity $\mathrm{sev}(t)$, then continues the \emph{same} per-trigger RNG stream to draw the 1{,}488-per-class extension at the \emph{same} $\mathrm{sev}(t)$; both sizes therefore sample the same proposal-time target mixture and differ only in nominal candidate evidence (the analysis verifies prefix-hash equality and severity equality at every coupled proposal). \emph{Coupling scope, declared in advance:} the two always-deploy arms commit at every confirmed trigger and therefore share identical trigger and commit timelines --- their proposals are exactly coupled --- whereas gated arms may diverge after the first discordant decision, so gate$\times$size contrasts are seed-paired only.

\textbf{Estimands, families and outcome rules (all frozen).} G1 value of updating ($\mathrm{naive}_{512}-\mathrm{never}$ and $\mathrm{naive}_{2000}-\mathrm{never}$; 6 contrasts), G2 the primary size effect ($\mathrm{naive}_{2000}-\mathrm{naive}_{512}$; 3), G3 gate value at 2{,}000 (6), G4 secondary gate$\times$size interactions (6); Holm within family; CI90 equivalence at the same $\pm0.5$-point margin; the same guardrails. The historical P/A/E taxonomy does not apply to this question; the frozen classification of G2 per scenario is \textsc{size benefit} (Holm-significant, effect $\ge +0.5$), \textsc{size cost} (Holm-significant, $\le -0.5$), \textsc{no material size effect} (CI90 within $\pm0.5$) or otherwise unresolved/sub-material, with a program label \textsc{homogeneous} when all three scenarios share a class and \textsc{heterogeneous} otherwise. Future-value summaries are descriptive only and feed no rule.

\subsection{Promotion policies and the registered common-harness comparison}
\label{sec:baselines_method}

The primary policy matrix is common to every control: \textbf{never-adapt} (the frozen $h_0$), \textbf{always-deploy} (naive: commit every confirmed proposal), and the \textbf{point} and \textbf{strict} labeled-probe gates of \S\ref{sec:gates} (strict additionally rejects probe ties, at no additional labels). The formal sequential alternatives --- exact McNemar, the confidence-sequence gates and the assembled VBC-SG with its deferral and lifetime-spending machinery (\S\ref{sec:riskgates}) --- are evaluated as a separate axis under the historical frozen policy, where their guarantees and label costs are priced (\S\ref{sec:risk}).

\textbf{The registered common-harness comparison.} A final registered block evaluates published and reference alternatives under the final trusted configuration --- self-contained challengers on bit-identical raw streams. Its protocol was frozen before implementation and amended, still before any implementation or result, to place the \emph{primary} comparison at nominal 2{,}000-per-class evidence parity (the amendment is in the artifact). Policies: never-adapt, always-deploy, point and strict (anchors); \textbf{ATC} \cite{garg2022atc} and \textbf{DoC} \cite{guillory2021doc}, the two standard published label-free accuracy estimators, used as promotion rules (each model estimates its current-window accuracy from its own confidences plus a 512-row labeled validation sample drawn at its own training time; zero target-window labels); a \textbf{calibrated soft ensemble} and \textbf{replay 50/50} retraining (standard baselines); and the \texttt{river} reference implementations of \textbf{DDM} and \textbf{ADWIN} \cite{gama2004learning,bifet2007learning} as retraining triggers with always-deploy on fire (8 monitoring labels per window, 800 per stream). Selection criteria were frozen before any result: closeness to the same decision problem, public reproducibility, dataset compatibility, no information unavailable to competing methods, and checkable implementation fidelity. No faithfully reproducible end-to-end published adaptive-NIDS system was found that matched the same decision problem and information interface (\S\ref{sec:baselines}); none of the evaluated rows is labelled an adaptive-NIDS state-of-the-art method. Six scenarios (three benchmarks $\times$ full and zero drift), 96 arms on 30 fresh seeds (5001--5030): the nine policies at 2{,}000/class plus a secondary 512/class sensitivity block for \{naive, point, strict, ATC, DoC, ensemble\} (DDM/ADWIN and replay are not duplicated there: their candidate construction equals naive's, or --- for replay --- size is a compound intervention). Information budgets differ legitimately by method definition and are documented, not equalized (Supplementary \S S10).

\textbf{Families and rules (frozen).} Primary, at 2{,}000/class: PF1 zero-drift loss avoidance and PF2 full-drift benefit retention (each of \{ATC, DoC, ensemble, replay, DDM, ADWIN\} minus naive; 18 contrasts each), PF3 the published estimators minus the point gate (12). Secondary: SF4 the 512/class sensitivity (18) and SF5 method$\times$size interactions (30; seed-paired). Holm within family; per-cell classification \textsc{material gain} / \textsc{material cost} (Holm-significant, $|\Delta| \ge 0.5$), \textsc{compatible} (CI90 within $\pm0.5$) or \textsc{unresolved}; anchor-versus-anchor contrasts are descriptive here so that the registered hypotheses of \S\ref{sec:sizematched_method}--\S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift_method} are not double-tested. Four registered statements (S1--S4, Supplementary \S S10) are evaluated literally.

\subsection{Outcomes and inference}

Per window we record balanced accuracy (BA) and attack-class F1; per stream we average over the 100 windows, and per condition over seeds. BA is the registered primary endpoint and measures the mean effect; attack recall and false-positive rate are preregistered non-inferiority guardrails that restrict the interpretation (they gate safety \emph{language}, not the registered verdicts); harmful-proposal summaries are descriptive within-trajectory fractions over seed-clustered commits; probe-level guarantees are not future-deployment guarantees. We report \textbf{gain vs. no-adaptation} (the frozen $h_0$ baseline), \textbf{committed adaptations} and \textbf{labels used}. To quantify the headroom of the decision we define a per-stream \textbf{policy oracle}, $\mathrm{BA}_{\text{oracle}}=\mathbb{E}_{\text{seed}}\max(\mathrm{BA}_{\text{no-adapt}},\mathrm{BA}_{\text{detector}})$ --- a conservative lower bound on the value of perfect per-trigger decisions --- the \textbf{regret} of naive triggering $\mathrm{BA}_{\text{oracle}}-\mathrm{BA}_{\text{naive}}$, and the \textbf{harm frequency} (fraction of streams on which adapting scored below no-adaptation). All confirmatory comparisons are \textbf{truly paired}: on the v2 harness every policy processes bit-identical pre-generated streams, and contrasts use cluster (per-seed) bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals; numbers from the initial exploratory study use seed-matched bootstrap CIs (conservative rather than fully paired).

\textbf{Evidence stages.} The study combines an exploratory discovery stage, the registered frozen-policy replication, two registered comparability controls (transformer ownership; zero-drift candidate size), the registered full-drift size control, the registered common-harness comparison, supporting registered analyses, and chronological boundary evaluations. These stages have different epistemic roles; the primary conclusions rely on the self-contained-pipeline, size-matched and common-harness blocks, while the remaining blocks test mechanisms, costs or boundaries. The full evidence hierarchy, per-block configuration, seeds and provenance are catalogued in Supplementary \S S0.

\subsection{The historical frozen configuration: where the promotion problem appeared}
\label{sec:historical}

Under the program's historical configuration --- a frozen, incumbent-owned scaler and PCA reused by every challenger, and 512-per-class challengers against a 2{,}000-per-class incumbent --- a registered replication on the hardened harness (seeds 104--133; full table in Supplementary \S S2.11) established the reference behaviour that motivated everything that follows: naive always-deploy updating is net-harmful in the harm regime ($-1.64$ balanced-accuracy points on ToN-IoT) and the 32-label commit gate converts it to a net benefit ($+0.79$; $+2.43$ above naive) while preserving the benefit and marginal regimes, under both a classical and a quantum-kernel detector. The same configuration makes always-deploy net-harmful in all three benchmarks once the incumbent stays healthy --- under mild drift ($-0.46$, $-0.15$, $-0.65$; a prediction locked before running) and under zero drift with random proposals ($-2.76$, $-0.75$, $-4.75$; Supplementary \S S2.12) --- and the residual zero-drift loss is recovered almost entirely by a statistical commit rule that refuses to commit without evidence.

Read on its own, this block says ``promotion harms a healthy incumbent, and a gate rescues it''. The rest of the paper shows why that reading is a statement about the configuration: both of its asymmetries --- who owns the preprocessing, and how much evidence the challenger sees --- turn out to drive the harm and, with it, most of the gate's measured value. The frozen-policy numbers remain valid within their configuration and are retained as diagnostic evidence, not as the final account of comparable challengers (their full grids: Supplementary \S S2.11--S2.12).

\subsection{Candidate construction: preprocessing ownership changes the apparent value of promotion}
\label{sec:symmetric}

Table~\ref{tab:symmetric_pipeline} reports the replication of \S\ref{sec:symmetric_method}; its registered verdict was Scenario~A --- \emph{persistence with a shifted locus}: preprocessing asymmetry amplified harmful promotion, while a residual zero-drift harm survived for self-contained challengers. All of its challengers train on 512 flows per class against a 2{,}000/class incumbent --- the historical configuration whose consequences \S\ref{sec:sizematched} then isolates. Three findings, in the order the frozen families test them; the fourth registered family (gate value) is read in \S\ref{sec:gatevalue}.

\textbf{(1) The mean full-drift harm does not persist under self-contained pipelines.} With each challenger owning its preprocessing, always-deploy is \emph{beneficial} in all three full-drift regimes: PortScan $+7.21$ [4.89, 9.52], UNSW-Recon $+2.55$ [2.34, 2.75], and --- the regime where the harm was originally reported --- ToN-IoT $+1.03$ [0.55, 1.53], all Holm-significant.

\textbf{(2) Preprocessing ownership is a major amplifier --- not a complete explanation of candidate-promotion risk.} The registered interaction $\mathrm{BA}_{\text{naive,own}} - \mathrm{BA}_{\text{naive,frozen}}$, on bit-identical raw streams and candidate batches, is positive everywhere it was harmful: $+5.98$ [3.82, 8.26] on ToN-IoT full, $+0.90$ and $+1.15$ on PortScan and UNSW full, and $+5.49$/$+2.36$ on the ToN/PortScan zero-drift streams. The frozen configuration had been handicapping every challenger, most severely exactly where the harm was reported. The original full-drift harm was therefore configuration-dependent and strongly amplified by incumbent-owned preprocessing: switching to self-contained preprocessing removes the observed mean full-drift harm and accounts for the large frozen-versus-own difference. Freezing a representation can be a legitimate production policy; the issue is generalizing results obtained under that treatment beyond it.

\textbf{(3) But ownership does not explain all of the risk: zero-drift promotion harm persists at the historical candidate size.} With no drift at all, self-contained 512-per-class challengers still hurt: naive$-$never is $-1.74$ [$-2.35$, $-0.96$] on PortScan and $-0.65$ [$-0.77$, $-0.55$] on UNSW-Recon --- both beyond the preregistered $\pm0.5$-point materiality margin, Holm-significant --- and $-0.38$ [$-0.53$, $-0.25$] on ToN-IoT, sub-material but significant and \emph{not} equivalent to zero (its CI90 is not inside $\pm0.5$). These challengers were also trained on a quarter of the incumbent's evidence, and \S\ref{sec:sizematched} shows that asymmetry, not re-estimation per se, accounts for the mean effect.

\input{tables/table_symmetric_pipeline.tex}

\textbf{Security guardrails and future-negative signs.} Every BA-winning own-transformer gate cell passes both preregistered non-inferiority margins except one: on zero-drift UNSW-Recon, strict validation improves balanced accuracy and false-positive rate, but its attack-recall lower confidence bound crosses the preregistered 1-point margin ($\Delta$recall $-0.99$, bound $-1.26$; it passes the lax $-2.0$ sensitivity), and that cell is not described as a security improvement anywhere in this paper; the FPR point estimate improves in each of the other winning cells (up to $-3.33$ points for strict on zero-drift PortScan), while several non-winning full-drift gate cells fail the FPR margin (full panel in Supplementary \S S7). Under own-transformer naive updating at 512/class, the descriptive fraction of committed proposals with future-negative five-window sign is 65\%/61\%/46\% on the zero-drift streams (42\% on ToN full), with a clearly negative mean; commits cluster within seeds and support no population-prevalence or deployment-probability inference. The quantum monitors, the sequential VBC-SG frontier and the mild-drift matrix were not re-run under own-transformer pipelines; their evidence remains scoped to the frozen policy (\S7).

\subsection{Candidate evidence at zero drift: nominal candidate-evidence size accounts for the residual zero-drift mean harm}
\label{sec:sizematched}

Table~\ref{tab:size_matched} reports the registered control of \S\ref{sec:sizematched_method}, and it resolves the residual attribution question within this control. Two findings here, one per frozen damage family; the gate family (F3) is read in \S\ref{sec:gatevalue}.

\textbf{(1) The 512 harm replicates; matched-size means are compatible with the registered margin.} On thirty entirely fresh seeds, 512-per-class challengers reproduce the replication's residual harm almost exactly (naive$_{512}-$never: $-1.70$ [$-2.05$, $-1.36$] PortScan, $-0.65$ [$-0.77$, $-0.55$] UNSW, $-0.24$ [$-0.39$, $-0.10$] ToN --- descriptive, uncorrected). Give the same challengers the incumbent's 2{,}000 flows per class --- as a pure extension of the identical nested batch --- and naive$_{2000}-$never is $+0.19$ [$-0.15$, $+0.55$], $-0.02$ [$-0.10$, $+0.06$] and $-0.01$ [$-0.05$, $+0.04$]. The three mean effects are compatible with the preregistered $\pm0.5$-point margin because their CI90s lie inside it. PortScan is margin-sensitive at $\pm0.2$: its CI90 upper bound is $0.494$, only $0.006$ points inside the registered margin, so that cell is margin-dependent and does not demonstrate absence of an effect; UNSW-NB15 and ToN-IoT pass the $\pm0.2$ sensitivity, and all three pass at $\pm1.0$. None of the F1 damage contrasts approaches Holm significance.


\textbf{(2) Candidate size is the driver, and it is Holm-significant everywhere.} The paired size effect naive$_{2000}-$naive$_{512}$ is $+1.89$ [$+1.54$, $+2.23$] on PortScan, $+0.63$ [$+0.53$, $+0.73$] on UNSW and $+0.23$ [$+0.10$, $+0.38$] on ToN --- essentially the mirror image of the 512 harm, all three Holm-significant. Increasing candidate evidence made each mean effect compatible with the registered margin in the evaluated zero-drift control; it did not, and cannot, establish absence of an effect or equivalence in every respect a proposal can differ (full tables in Supplementary \S S8).

\textbf{What nominal sample-size parity does and does not match.} The matched condition equates the challenger's and the incumbent's \emph{nominal} per-class training size: 2{,}000 rows per class against 2{,}000 rows per class. It does not establish equality in effective sample size, temporal coverage, temporal diversity, subtype support, duplication, label quality, prevalence or information content. Pool draws are with replacement, so nominal rows can contain duplicates and should not be read as effective sample sizes; the candidate batch is a controlled balanced sample from the current-regime pool, not a time-ordered slice of observed traffic; and neither condition equates the temporal span the two training sets cover. Within these controlled conditions the experiment isolates the nominal row-count factor, and that factor accounts for the residual mean harm. Nominal parity is not information parity: evidence comparability in deployment remains an operational property to be audited, and \S7 records the corresponding scope limits.

\input{tables/table_size_matched.tex}

\textbf{Registered outcome: ATTENUATION --- and what it does and does not mean.} The frozen rules classify this result mechanically. PERSISTENCE fails because there is no material mean damage at the matched size (no F1 contrast is $\le -0.5$ with CI95 below zero). ELIMINATION fails on exactly one criterion: the preregistered harmful-future-value check (E3) keys on the \emph{sign rate} of committed proposals' five-window value, and at the matched size that rate is $\approx$48--52\% in two benchmarks --- so the registered outcome is ATTENUATION, and we keep it. That criterion deserves a candid gloss: when the mean future value of committed challengers is approximately zero --- which is what near-exchangeable challengers make expected (\S\ref{sec:sizematched_method}) --- negative and positive signs occur at close to equal frequency, so a sign-rate threshold of this form is structurally unable to certify elimination in exactly the situation it was registered to detect. The label is therefore preserved mechanically, as the frozen rules require, and should be read as a property of the registered rule, not as evidence of directional residual harm. The same logs show a small mean five-window future value of committed 2{,}000-challengers ($-0.06$ to $+0.03$ points, against $-0.13$ to $-1.03$ at 512): a near-50\% negative-sign rate must be read as proposal-level variability within trajectories, not a deployment-risk probability. The formal classification and the margin-based mean comparison are distinct; neither demonstrates absence of an effect, and the registered full-drift control of the next subsection does not retroactively change this historical classification.

\textbf{Guardrails at the matched size.} All six matched-size gate cells pass both preregistered non-inferiority margins vs.\ naive$_{2000}$ (paired $\Delta$recall one-sided lower bounds all $>-1.0$; $\Delta$FPR upper bounds all $<+0.5$), while at 512 the zero-drift UNSW strict cell again fails recall non-inferiority --- replicating the earlier trade-off on fresh seeds (full guardrail table in Supplementary \S S8). There is no security trade-off at the matched size; there is also no BA gain, and the absence of a trade-off is not evidence of utility.

\subsection{Candidate evidence under real drift: more nominal evidence improves promotion in all three benchmarks}
\label{sec:sizematched_drift}

The zero-drift control leaves open the objection that its null was expected of any correct implementation, because a 2{,}000-per-class challenger drawn from the incumbent's own pools at severity zero is close to an exchangeable copy of the incumbent. The registered full-drift control of \S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift_method} answers it in the only regime where candidate evidence is a non-trivial hypothesis: the challenger now samples a drifted mixture the incumbent has never seen. Table~\ref{tab:size_matched_drift} reports it (21 arms, seeds 6001--6030; every arm authorized-mode on a clean source tree; nesting and severity audits verified at all 707 coupled proposals; the two always-deploy arms proposal-coupled in 90/90 seed-scenarios).

\textbf{(1) Registered primary result: a homogeneous, material size benefit.} The paired size effect naive$_{2000}-$naive$_{512}$ is $+0.82$ [$0.67$, $0.98$] on PortScan, $+1.66$ [$1.51$, $1.81$] on UNSW-Recon and $+1.00$ [$0.60$, $1.39$] on ToN-IoT --- all Holm-significant (adjusted $p \approx 3\times10^{-5}$) and all above the $+0.5$-point materiality margin. The frozen classification is \textsc{size benefit} in each scenario, so the registered program outcome is \textsc{homogeneous-size benefit}. Always-deploy is beneficial at both candidate sizes (G1: $+8.90$/$+2.13$/$+1.44$ at 512 and $+9.72$/$+3.79$/$+2.44$ at 2{,}000 over never-adapt; all Holm-significant), and the larger candidate simply captures more of the available recovery.

\textbf{(2) What this establishes, and what it does not.} Within the evaluated domain --- pool-based progressive covariate drift, balanced nested candidate batches drawn at the proposal-time mixture, SVC-RBF with self-contained preprocessing, KS-max triggering --- increasing nominal challenger evidence improves promotion outcomes not only under the zero-drift negative control but also under actual drift. This substantially weakens the objection that the zero-drift size result was merely a consequence of exchangeable same-distribution re-draws: the same nested intervention, where the challenger is not exchangeable with the incumbent, produces a material and homogeneous effect. It also resolves the frozen-transformer precedent in which size-matching deepened full-drift harm: that over-specialization pattern was bound to the frozen incumbent-owned representation and does not reproduce under self-contained pipelines. The result does \emph{not} establish effective information parity, a universal monotonic benefit from more data, causal generality beyond the evaluated nested sampling design, or that larger training windows improve arbitrary adaptive systems; nominal rows drawn with replacement remain nominal, and \S7 states the corresponding limits.

\input{tables/table_size_matched_drift.tex}

\textbf{(3) Validation at the matched size under drift.} Point and strict validation add no positive average value at 2{,}000/class in any of the six registered G3 contrasts: the point gate is $+0.15$, $-0.06$ and $+0.01$ over always-deploy (none Holm-significant) and the strict gate $-0.00$, $-0.34$ and $-0.14$, where the UNSW-Recon strict cell is a \emph{resolved cost} ($-0.34$ [$-0.53$, $-0.15$], Holm-significant). The secondary gate$\times$size interactions are $\le 0$ in every point estimate (UNSW strict $-0.31$, Holm-significant). Guardrails, exactly as registered: every matched-size point cell passes both recall and FPR non-inferiority; strict at 2{,}000 fails the FPR margin on UNSW-Recon ($\Delta$FPR $+0.86$, upper bound above $+0.5$), so no safety language attaches to that cell; at 512 (descriptive cells) strict fails the recall margin on PortScan and ToN-IoT. The descriptive 512-side gate cells show the only resolved gain of this block: the point gate on ToN-IoT full drift ($+0.46$ [$0.09$, $0.88$] over naive$_{512}$), consistent with validation earning its keep where the challenger is evidence-disadvantaged (full matrices and guardrails in Supplementary \S S9).

\subsection{Validation has conditional average value}
\label{sec:gatevalue}

The registered gate families of the three comparability controls, read together, locate where validation earns its keep --- and where it measurably does not.

\textbf{Under a nominal-evidence disadvantage, validation retains material value.} At own-transformer 512/class in the symmetric replication (seeds 3001--3030), point and strict validation improve over naive in all six zero-drift comparisons (Holm-significant), recovering much or nearly all of the loss depending on the cell: strict gains $+1.68$ [1.23, 2.13] over naive on PortScan, $+0.51$ [0.38, 0.64] on UNSW, $+0.34$ [0.20, 0.49] on ToN (point $+0.75/+0.19/+0.11$). ``Recover'' means improvement \emph{over always-deploy}: strict returns to within $0.06$--$0.14$ points of never-adapt in all three regimes, the point gate still sits $0.27$--$0.99$ points below it, and no zero-drift cell converts to a net benefit over never adapting --- nor should it where there is nothing to gain. At full drift the point gate still filters harmful candidates on ToN-IoT ($+0.64$ [0.26, 1.06]); the costs are equally visible: the gates add nothing material on PortScan ($+0.19$/$+0.05$, n.s.) and the point gate has a small resolved \emph{cost} on UNSW-Recon ($-0.21$ [$-0.36$, $-0.06$]) --- where updating is already clearly beneficial, validation's marginal value can be zero or slightly negative (full contrasts in Supplementary \S S7).

\textbf{At nominal evidence parity, validation has nothing left to recover --- at zero drift and under drift alike.} All six F3 gate contrasts at the matched size under zero drift are tiny ($+0.01$ to $+0.13$ points, every one below $0.14$), none Holm-significant, and every one CI90-equivalent to naive within $\pm0.5$; the secondary F4 interactions are uniformly negative and Holm-significant (e.g.\ strict on PortScan $-1.54$ [$-1.89$, $-1.20$]) --- the gates' value at 512 was, to the extent the interaction measures it, compensation for the challenger's evidence disadvantage (Supplementary \S S8). Under full drift the registered G3 family repeats the pattern with fresh seeds: 0/6 positive gate effects at 2{,}000/class and a resolved strict-gate cost on UNSW-Recon (\S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift}). No average benefit under these controls is not the same as no value: it does not rule out value against individual harmful candidates, tail risk, temporally uncertain streams, or configurations not evaluated here.

\textbf{Evidence versus validation: what each nominal label buys.} The same sealed outputs answer the operator's trade-off: more candidate-training evidence, or a small challenger plus a validation probe? Table~\ref{tab:evidence_validation_tradeoff} compares, per benchmark, the 512-per-class policies against naive at nominal 2{,}000-per-class parity; its cells come from the size-matched control (seeds 4001--4030), so its 512-side gate values re-estimate, on fresh seeds, the same contrasts the first paragraph quoted from seeds 3001--3030 --- close but not identical numbers, from two registered runs. In nominal adjudication counts, moving from 512 to 2{,}000 samples per class requires $2\times1{,}488 = 2{,}976$ additional candidate-training labels per proposal, whereas the evaluated point/strict decision uses a 32-label probe. At 512, the strict gate recovers most of the mean loss (PortScan: naive$_{512}$ $-1.70$ vs strict$_{512}$ $-0.03$ against never, descriptive); at 2{,}000, the mean effects are compatible with the registered $\pm0.5$-point margin without a probe. This establishes no economic dominance in either direction: labels are counted, not priced, and the inspected-flow cost of acquiring balanced attack labels is not modeled --- under operational imbalance candidate-training and probe labels need not cost the same to obtain.

\input{tables/table_evidence_validation_tradeoff.tex}

Under the historical frozen policy the same conditionality appears from the other side: with a healthy incumbent and asymmetric challengers, point/strict and the risk-controlled rules recover most or all of the zero-drift loss, and a well-calibrated statistical gate commits nothing when there is nothing to gain (\S\ref{sec:v2}); the chronological replays (\S\ref{sec:boundaries}) show the complementary cost --- what conservative validation forgoes when the incumbent has collapsed.

\subsection{Registered common-harness comparison with published and reference baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:common_harness} reports the registered comparison of \S\ref{sec:baselines_method} at its primary condition --- self-contained challengers at nominal 2{,}000-per-class evidence parity, 96 arms on seeds 5001--5030, every policy on bit-identical streams. The rows are published generic methods (ATC, DoC), reference implementations (\texttt{river} DDM/ADWIN), standard baselines (calibrated ensemble, replay) and this paper's anchors; no faithfully reproducible end-to-end published adaptive-NIDS system was found that matched the same decision problem and information interface --- the per-sample rejection systems (Transcend, CADE) and continuous adapters (INSOMNIA) decide something else, and the cost-schedule formulations (CARA, Regol, IGPC-MSOS) require inputs their papers do not supply for this harness --- so their absence is stated, not used to imply superiority. Cross-policy information budgets differ by definition and are reported in Supplementary \S S10, never equalized.

\input{tables/table_common_harness.tex}

\textbf{Zero drift at parity: nothing left to rescue.} With 2{,}000-per-class challengers the always-deploy policy is already approximately neutral relative to never-adapt ($+0.25$, $+0.07$, $+0.02$; descriptive), while the 512-per-class anchor replicates the residual harm on a third fresh seed block ($-1.66$, $-0.58$, $-0.35$). Consequently no policy attains the registered statement S1 (``avoids the zero-drift loss''): every PF1 cell is \textsc{compatible} or \textsc{unresolved} except the calibrated ensemble on PortScan (\textsc{material gain}, $+1.46$). The zero-drift problem that label-free alternatives were previously priced against disappears once the challenger is comparably evidenced.

\textbf{Full drift at parity: two compatible alternatives, four that pay.} \textbf{ATC} is \textsc{compatible} with always-deploy on UNSW-Recon ($-0.22$) and ToN-IoT ($+0.10$) and \textsc{unresolved} on PortScan ($-0.65$); the \textbf{calibrated ensemble} is \textsc{compatible} in all three ($-0.09$, $-0.42$, $-0.23$). \textbf{DoC} pays a \textsc{material cost} on PortScan ($-2.59$) and UNSW-Recon ($-0.59$) and is \textsc{compatible} on ToN-IoT ($+0.21$); \textbf{replay} ($-0.89$, $-2.07$, $-1.22$), \textbf{river-DDM} ($-0.78$, $-2.67$, $-1.81$) and \textbf{river-ADWIN} ($-8.73$, $-4.06$, $-2.50$) are \textsc{material cost} in every full-drift scenario, the two monitors while also consuming 800 continuous monitoring labels per stream (ADWIN's cost is largely under-triggering: with its reference $\delta$ on eight labels per window it barely fires, so its arm stays close to never-adapt). Registered statement S2 (``pays for it at full drift'') therefore holds for DoC, replay, DDM and ADWIN and for neither ATC nor the ensemble. ATC is the strongest published zero-target-label competitor; the calibrated ensemble is the strongest standard label-free baseline (it commits every trigger and cannot decline an update, and it fails recall non-inferiority on PortScan). Compatibility here is the registered CI90-within-$\pm0.5$ criterion, not a demonstration of equality.

\textbf{Against the point gate.} PF3 compares the two published estimators with the point gate at parity: ATC is \textsc{compatible} with the gate on five of six scenarios and \textsc{unresolved} on PortScan full drift ($-0.73$), so statement S3 (``matches the point gate'') is narrowly not attained; DoC is a \textsc{material cost} relative to the gate on PortScan ($-2.67$) and UNSW-Recon ($-0.58$) full drift and \textsc{compatible} elsewhere. The exploratory-harness observation that DoC beat the point gate in the harm regime (Supplementary \S S1.5: $+1.16$ vs $+0.93$ at 512/class under the frozen policy) does not reproduce under the final harness at parity (ToN-IoT full drift: DoC$-$point $+0.01$, \textsc{compatible}); the registered evidence governs. Neither point nor strict validation dominates: their descriptive full-drift differences to always-deploy at 2{,}000 are $+0.08$/$-0.00$/$+0.20$ (point) and $-0.16$/$-0.23$/$-0.22$ (strict), the UNSW strict cell resolved negative, consistent with \S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift}. Many full-drift cells of the label-free and monitor policies fail the FPR non-inferiority guardrail (ADWIN $\Delta$FPR up to $+9.5$ points); the full panel is in Supplementary \S S10 and no safety language attaches to those cells.

\textbf{The 512/class sensitivity: method ordering depends on the candidate generator.} The secondary block is not an equal benchmark; it demonstrates something more useful. With evidence-disadvantaged 512-per-class challengers every label-free alternative gains at zero drift --- the ensemble $+2.93$/$+0.20$/$+0.36$, DoC $+1.93$/$+0.40$/$+0.44$, ATC $+1.65$/$+0.25$/$+0.26$ over naive$_{512}$ --- because naive$_{512}$ is itself harmful there, and ATC/DoC surrender full-drift benefit on PortScan ($-1.82$, $-3.63$) while gaining on ToN-IoT ($+0.48$, $+0.78$): the exploratory-harness texture, now on the final harness. All fifteen Holm-significant method$\times$size interactions (SF5) are negative, and the registered ordering-change rule S4 fires for ATC (PortScan zero drift: \textsc{material gain} at 512, \textsc{compatible} at 2{,}000) and for the calibrated ensemble (PortScan and ToN-IoT full drift: \textsc{material gain} at 512, \textsc{compatible} at 2{,}000); it does not fire for point, strict or DoC. The value of every evaluated safeguard --- labeled gates and label-free alternatives alike --- concentrates where candidate evidence is asymmetric. This is the paper's central methodological point stated in its sharpest form: even conclusions about \emph{which update policy appears preferable} depend on the comparability of the candidate generator being evaluated, so a policy ranking obtained with evidence-disadvantaged challengers need not survive at parity.

The historical blocks --- the frozen-policy comparison of Block~I, the frozen zero-drift control, the exploratory v1 estimates and the lifetime-budgeted frontier --- are retained in Supplementary \S S2.12 with their provenance and evidence tiers; where the same method and question are evaluated here, the registered common-harness evidence supersedes them, and cross-block comparisons remain descriptive. The comparison is a map of evaluated accuracy/label/update trade-offs, not a state-of-the-art ranking.

\textbf{Mechanism: update value tracks incumbent degradation, not detector score.} At each of 250 triggers the harness measured the incumbent's degradation over the five \emph{preceding} windows and the candidate-minus-incumbent BA over the five \emph{following} windows --- predictor and outcome share no algebraic term. Pre-trigger degradation predicts the future value of committing in every regime (r $= -$0.67 to $-$0.70, every clustered CI95 away from zero), while the detector's score at the same triggers shows no consistent association (r $= -$0.01 to $+$0.05, every CI95 straddling zero). \textbf{Supporting robustness.} \emph{Observed-data:} with every oracle privilege and leakage path removed --- candidate, probe and detector recalibration drawn from observed past traffic only, without replacement from a value-deduplicated pool --- the \textbf{leakage-free observed-data} arm reproduces the harm-regime rescue ($+$4.05 and $+$4.83 above naive on ToN-IoT full and mild drift). It is the only tier \textbf{free of simulator-oracle information} (Supplementary \S S2.7). \emph{Classifier and generator controls:} at the default 512/class budget the harmful zero-drift \emph{sign} appears for random forest, logistic regression, MLP and full-feature SVC, and it holds across replacement generators, the from-scratch cumulative generator being the deepest ($-$9.46 on ToN-IoT); size-matching removes it for the robust learners, localizing the residual magnitude to the fragile SVC-RBF pipeline (Supplementary \S S1.6, \S S2.12). \emph{Quantum detector:} the harm, the gate's rescue and the per-trigger mechanism all replicate under a quantum-kernel monitor at a ${\sim}114\times$ simulated cost --- a detector-invariance check, not a quantum-advantage claim (Supplementary \S S1.2).

\textbf{VBC-SG: what validating formally costs.} The sequential family of \S\ref{sec:riskgates} controls the per-proposal probability of falsely establishing probe-superiority; it does not bound future deployment harm. The deployment-long stratified guarantee is thus \textbf{non-vacuous within the evaluated} balanced-probe budget, from $b{=}256$ onward, at an abstention of 74--86\%. Empirically the risk-controlled gates committed \emph{zero observed} harmful updates over a complete 520-commit accounting (506 evaluable) against 39\% for always-deploy. These commits cluster within shared seeds and are \textbf{not treated as 506 independent Bernoulli trials}; because they are \emph{not independent trials}, no population-rate bound is inferred and ``zero observed'' is not ``eliminates''. The lesson: a \textbf{pooled sequential gate provides useful empirical risk control} at tens of adjudicated labels and captures the large benefits, while the \textbf{formally aligned stratified guarantee} requires substantially larger balanced-probe budgets. All of these numbers are evidence under the historical frozen-transformer policy; the frontier was not re-evaluated under the size-matched self-contained controls, where plain promotion carries no mean deficit at zero drift and a clear benefit under drift, and nothing here shows VBC-SG to be \emph{necessary} in that setting. We claim no mathematical novelty for the construction (Supplementary \S S2.9, \S S4).

\textbf{Chronological replays: the external boundary.} Thirteen registered replays on real, chronologically ordered streams (200 windows of 256 flows, strided to span each capture: ordering, labels and the temporal availability of every probe are real; the sampling density is not) delimit external support (Table~\ref{tab:chronological_q1}; per-replay detail in Supplementary \S S2.6). Every CICIDS incumbent collapses on a later window (no-adaptation BA 49--72\%) as each stream presents an unseen attack sub-type, and drift-triggered retraining recovers +13.6 to +43.8 BA points; on these deep-benefit streams the gate pays a real, metric-dependent premium ($-$5.0 to $-$15.1 of the recovered balanced-accuracy points on three streams; nothing on Wednesday), while on overall accuracy it beats naive on the same streams, and a registered interventional test rules out probe class composition as the cause (probe \emph{staleness} remains the hypothesis consistent with the data). Where the incumbent stays healthy --- the two raw UNSW-NB15 timelines (82.9\% and 84.2\% BA) --- updating helps moderately and the point and strict gates have higher point estimates than always-deploying (strict $+2.78$ and $+1.34$ descriptive, committing 2.5 and 3.6 times per stream against naive's 13.0 and 17.3), while the healthy Wednesday intra-day split is an unresolved counterexample with the gates $0.5$--$0.7$ points \emph{below} naive. These are descriptive point-estimate comparisons, not direct inferential superiority claims: the registered chronological multiplicity family tests strict against no-adaptation, not strict against always-deploying --- a registration that is structurally easy to satisfy wherever the incumbent collapses (any policy that commits at all clears a collapsed baseline), so the family's confirmatory content is confined to the healthy-incumbent replays. \textbf{Net harm remains unobserved}: on no chronological stream does always-deploying lose to never adapting, so the harmful-update finding stays a controlled-condition result; the replays estimate no deployment harm frequency, and ToN-IoT ships no timestamps, so the controlled harm benchmark has no chronological analogue. The price of that conservatism must be stated with the same prominence: on the five CICIDS replays VBC-SG-Cohort-sim commits at most once per stream and retains only 0--9\% of always-deploying's recovery ($+0.16$ of $+14.78$ on Tue$\to$Wed+Thu+Fri, $+0.91$ of $+35.29$ on Wed$\to$Fri, $+1.56$ of $+36.07$, $+0.56$ of $+6.47$, $0.00$ of $+0.88$), while spending up to ${\sim}444$ adjudicated labels per stream where it buys evidence; the point and strict gates retain 73--100\% on the same streams (48\% for strict on the shallow Thursday intra-day split). On the two healthy UNSW timelines the same policy retains 91\% and 129\% of always-deploying's gain, at 1{,}232 and 1{,}634 labels per stream. Formal lifetime risk control can become extremely conservative exactly where the incumbent has the most recovery headroom; this is the operational price of the guarantee, and Table~\ref{tab:chronological_q1} should be read with it.

\textbf{Operational feasibility.} Every count above is an \emph{adjudicated} label; an operator pays in \emph{inspected flows}, and finding an \emph{attack} label at operating prevalence costs about $1/\pi$ inspections. A registered acquisition-yield simulation prices that search: alert-enriched inspection cuts it 5--8$\times$ at every prevalence, while the commit rule itself is shown only an independent uniform validation sample (32 adjudications), so enrichment improves discovery without touching decision validity. The arm is deliberately bounded --- candidate batch and detector calibration remain balanced, and it is not an end-to-end deployment cost. A separate prevalence sensitivity shows the gate holds down to $\pi \approx 0.05$ but its harm-regime protection dissolves at $\pi = 0.01$, where two-thirds of 32-flow probes contain no attack; there the binding constraint is upstream --- a starved \emph{trigger}, not the gate (Supplementary \S S2.3). The historical frozen-policy diagnostic further shows that simply rejecting probe ties (\emph{reject-ties}) recovers most of the residual zero-drift loss at no additional labels --- a genuine Pareto point within that configuration (Supplementary \S S2.12).

\section{Discussion}


The program --- historical diagnostic, ownership replication, zero-drift and full-drift size controls, common-harness comparison, chronological matrix --- answers three questions.

\textbf{Q1: What makes an adaptive challenger comparable, and why does it change the apparent value of promotion?} A challenger cannot be evaluated on comparable terms if it inherits an incumbent-owned representation or receives substantially less training evidence. Both asymmetries were latent in the historical configuration, and each was isolated by a preregistered control: frozen incumbent-owned preprocessing accounted for the mean full-drift harm (the ownership interaction reaches $+6.0$ points where the harm was reported), and the challengers' 4$\times$ nominal per-class evidence disadvantage accounts for the residual zero-drift mean harm (all size effects Holm-significant; matched-size mean effects compatible with $\pm0.5$ points in 3/3 under the zero-drift control). The full-drift size control then shows that candidate evidence is not only a zero-drift artifact of exchangeable re-draws: with a drifted target the same 512$\to$2{,}000 intervention adds a material, homogeneous $+0.82$ to $+1.66$ points to promotion outcomes. The mechanism is concrete: a challenger scored in a representation fitted on someone else's data, or fitted to a quarter of the incumbent's evidence, replaces a model built on more information, and the generalization loss can outweigh the gain from recency; whether that pushes the outcome below no-adaptation depends on the downstream model's robustness --- SVC-RBF is fragile enough on ToN-IoT to go net-negative, whereas random forest, logistic regression and MLP stay non-negative (Supplementary \S S1.6). In the evaluated setting, what looked like a property of drift-triggered promotion was, in its mean, a property of how the candidates were built and evidenced. The controls identify nominal sample-size and representation effects in the evaluated pool-based setting; broader evidence comparability --- temporal coverage, diversity, subtype support, label quality, effective sample size --- remains an operational property, not a consequence of equal row counts alone.

\textbf{Q2: When does validation add value?} Validation has its greatest average value when candidate comparability is weak or candidate quality is uncertain. Under the frozen transformer the gate was partly compensating a representation bias; at own-transformer 512/class it compensated a nominal-evidence disadvantage (its value there is real --- up to $+1.68$ points --- and guardrail-clean in all but one cell). At matched 2{,}000-per-class evidence the picture is uniform: at zero drift no detectable mean gate benefit (all six contrasts $<0.14$ points, none significant, interactions uniformly negative), and under full drift no positive gate effect in 0/6 cells with a measurable strict-gate cost on UNSW-Recon ($-0.34$). The current experiments therefore do not support universal validation, and we do not call validation necessary, generally safer, superior, or the recommended default. Where it earns its keep, the gate works because it estimates a contemporaneous surrogate for the quantity the mechanism identifies --- the sign of the candidate--incumbent difference --- from a small labeled probe whose validity does not depend on the model being judged; the realized future value remains observable only downstream. A defensible \emph{operational} interpretation, which extrapolates beyond the fixed-policy evidence and is labelled as such, is to validate when upstream comparability cannot be established cheaply or when tail-risk or governance requirements justify the additional caution --- and to treat a demonstrably comparable, well-evidenced challenger as one that validation will mostly delay. The empirical evidence is confined to the fixed-policy arms evaluated; we did not evaluate a meta-controller that selects a gate or its strength online.

The label bill is layered: retraining labels are unavoidable for any labeled-retraining loop; carving the probe from that same (deduplicated) batch costs nothing extra and buys most of the harm reduction where harm exists; converting average harm into average benefit in the controlled confirmatory harm regime costs a fresh probe of $\approx$32 flows per confirmed drift under the controlled balanced-probe protocol --- adjudication counts compatible with analyst-assisted workflows, though the inspected-flow acquisition cost of the balanced candidate batch and of the formal stratified probes was not evaluated. Under the historical frozen policy the same conditionality appears from the other side: a well-calibrated statistical gate commits nothing when there is nothing to gain, and on collapsed chronological incumbents every conservative layer forfeits part of a large recovery --- VBC-SG almost all of it (\S\ref{sec:boundaries}). Where degradation is strong and well established, excessive validation delays beneficial adaptation, and the operator's protection is not more gating but bounded deferral, adequate probe budgets, and rollback capability.

\textbf{Q3: What does the registered comparison say about alternative update policies?} Different alternatives occupy different information/accuracy/update-cost trade-offs, and none dominates. At parity under full drift, ATC and the calibrated ensemble are compatible with always-deploy while DoC, replay and the reference monitors pay material costs; the monitors additionally require continuous monitoring labels and, in this harness, fire either harmfully (DDM) or barely at all (ADWIN). ATC is compatible with the point gate on five of six scenarios at zero target labels, which is the strongest case in this study for a published label-free promotion rule; DoC's exploratory-harness advantage over the gate does not survive the final harness, a reminder that exploratory rankings should not govern where registered final-harness evidence exists. The 512/class sensitivity then delivers the study's sharpest methodological result: all fifteen resolved method$\times$size interactions are negative, and the registered ordering-change rule fires for ATC and the ensemble --- the apparent preference among update policies depends on whether the candidate generator being evaluated is comparable. Any benchmark that ranks adaptation policies with evidence-disadvantaged or asymmetrically constructed challengers is ranking the asymmetry as much as the policies.

\textbf{Implications for operational NIDS, and what is not implied.} Detection is not decision: a detector reports that the distribution moved, the update decision needs the sign of the candidate--incumbent difference, and the evaluated detectors' scores showed no consistent association with it within triggered decisions (r $\approx$ +0.06; Supplementary \S S1.1); improving the detector --- including a ${\sim}114\times$ more expensive quantum-kernel monitor --- did not change any promotion outcome we evaluated. The controlled harm results license no prevalence claim about production: net harmful always-deploy behaviour was not observed in the chronological replays evaluated, so harmful promotion should be treated as a risk concentrated in identifiable conditions --- asymmetric construction, limited candidate evidence, healthy incumbents --- not as a documented operational regularity. The evaluation discipline generalizes: adaptation benchmarks must control challenger representation and training evidence \emph{before} attributing harm or benefit to a promotion policy, or ranking policies, because asymmetric construction can produce either. We offer the construction $\to$ comparability $\to$ validation control sequence as a template for evaluating adaptive pipelines beyond intrusion detection, while stressing that our evidence is confined to network-security benchmarks.

\textbf{An operational design hypothesis.} The full combination --- a self-contained challenger, nominal size matching, observed-data construction, a real alarm process and natural prevalence --- was not evaluated jointly. The sequence below is therefore a design hypothesis derived from separate controls, not a demonstrated deployment policy:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Audit candidate construction.} Do not treat incumbent-owned frozen preprocessing as a neutral evaluation convention. When candidate comparability is intended, use a self-contained challenger that fits its own preprocessing on its own training batch; if production deliberately freezes the incumbent's representation, state and validate that ownership policy explicitly (\S\ref{sec:symmetric}).
  \item \emph{Audit evidence comparability.} Match the challenger's nominal training evidence to the incumbent's, or treat any shortfall as a reason to validate (\S\ref{sec:sizematched}, \S\ref{sec:sizematched_drift}); nominal parity is necessary for a fair reading, not sufficient for full comparability.
  \item \emph{Assess incumbent health.} Degradation, not detector score, predicts update value (\S\ref{sec:v2}); incumbent health should inform the promotion decision, although the cost and calibration of such monitoring were not evaluated here.
  \item \emph{Condition validation strength on uncertainty.} Validate conservatively when candidate quality or incumbent health is uncertain; when the challenger is comparable and degradation is strong and well established, avoid unnecessary delay --- conservative gates measurably forfeit recovery there (\S\ref{sec:boundaries}).
  \item \emph{Monitor post-promotion outcomes and retain rollback capability.} Mean compatibility within a margin is not a per-proposal guarantee; the tail and sign-rate evidence (\S\ref{sec:v2}, \S\ref{sec:sizematched}) is exactly why post-deployment monitoring stays in the loop.
\end{enumerate}
Under this design hypothesis, and conditional on a comparable proposal being generated, the detector acts mainly as a scheduler; at extreme class imbalance, detector calibration and alarm starvation determine whether any of this is invoked at all.

\textbf{Computational overhead of self-contained challengers (operational implication, not a benchmark).} A self-contained challenger refits its own preprocessing bundle --- here a feature standardizer and an 8-component PCA --- on its candidate batch before training the classifier, and a commit deploys the complete bundle; inference cost after deployment is structurally similar under both policies, since each served flow passes through one scaler, one projection and one classifier either way. We did not run an isolated timing study of the two policies. The only measured figures are the coarse per-arm wall-clock times recorded with each confirmatory arm (sequential single-machine runs that include stream generation, detector scoring, candidate training and evaluation): across the 18 matched scenario$\times$policy pairs of the symmetric-pipeline replication, own-transformer arms took on average $0.97\times$ the wall-clock of their frozen counterparts (range $0.89$--$1.06$), so the transformer refit is not visible at whole-arm granularity; the 2{,}000-per-class arms of the size-matched control took $1.2$--$1.5\times$ the wall-clock of their 512-per-class counterparts --- consistent with SVC-RBF training and scoring cost growing with the batch, though a whole-arm measurement cannot isolate the cause. These single-run, whole-arm timings do not isolate preprocessing overhead, and that an own-transformer arm occasionally runs \emph{faster} than its frozen counterpart is run-to-run noise, not a speedup caused by self-contained preprocessing. They are operational context, not a benchmark, and they are distinct from the ${\sim}114\times$ simulated overhead of the quantum-kernel monitor (\S\ref{sec:v2}), which concerns the drift detector rather than the challenger pipeline.


\section{Limitations}


\textbf{Benchmarks and drift construction.} Three public benchmarks in binary benign-versus-attack form, with scenarios constructed from regime pools, do not constitute broad external validation; the strongest benefit evidence is concentrated in CICIDS2017, and the marginal and harmful ends come from UNSW-NB15 and ToN-IoT. The core drift trajectories are gradual mixing ramps between real regime pools --- covariate/regime drift; we do not isolate a $p(y\,|\,x)$ change, and recurrent drift is untested. Candidate generation is pool-based and controlled: candidate batches and probes are sampled at the true current severity, information a production system lacks; the registered leakage-free observed-data arm (\S\ref{sec:v2}; Supplementary \S S2.7) reproduces the harmful-regime rescue from observed past traffic only, but equivalence to the oracle-probe arm was not tested and the size-matched controls were not repeated in observed-data form.

\textbf{Learners and pipelines.} The primary pipeline is SVC-RBF with an 8-dimensional PCA chosen for quantum-simulation tractability. At full drift the historical net-harm result is specific to that fragile classifier --- the fragile-model tail of the evaluated learner spectrum --- and does not appear for more robust classifiers (Supplementary \S S1.6); the classifier-, generator- and size-dependence of the zero-drift replacement harm is summarized in \S\ref{sec:v2}; incremental or weighted online learners with no discrete replacement step remain untested, and the full-drift size control and the common-harness comparison were run on SVC-RBF only. Neither the frozen-policy full-drift harm nor the 512-challenger zero-drift harm should be quoted as a general property of drift-triggered updating: harm is what happens when a healthy model is replaced by a challenger whose construction or evidence is materially asymmetric --- a condition on the proposal, not on the dataset.

\textbf{Nominal evidence is not effective information.} The size-matched controls equate \emph{nominal} per-class sample size; temporal coverage, diversity, subtype support, label quality, duplication, prevalence and effective sample size are not equated by equal row counts, and with-replacement pool draws mean nominal rows can contain duplicates (\S\ref{sec:sizematched}). The zero-drift control's null is the outcome expected of near-exchangeable challengers; the full-drift control establishes a material size benefit only within its pool-based nested design, and we make no claim that larger candidate batches always help arbitrary nonstationary systems or that the benefit is monotone beyond the two evaluated sizes. The trigger of the zero-drift control is a random proposal rate, not a real alarm process. The complete self-contained $+$ size-matched $+$ observed-data $+$ real-alarm $+$ natural-prevalence combination was not evaluated jointly, so the operational sequence in \S6 is a design hypothesis derived from separate controls. Mean compatibility at the matched size (with the margin-dependent PortScan zero-drift cell) does not demonstrate absence of an effect, nor imply that no individual committed candidate can be worse than the incumbent; the future-negative sign percentages (65\%/61\% at 512, 48--52\% at the matched size, at $H{=}5$ under zero drift) are descriptive within-trajectory fractions over seed-clustered commits, not population prevalences or deployment probabilities.

\textbf{The common-harness comparison.} Information budgets differ legitimately by method definition --- ATC and DoC use a 512-row training-time validation sample per model and zero target labels, DDM/ADWIN consume 800 continuous monitoring labels per stream, the gates 32 target labels per decision --- and are reported rather than equalized; policies with probability outputs train Platt-calibrated SVCs, which leaves the classifiers' predictions unchanged. No end-to-end external adaptive-NIDS system was reproduced, for the reasons stated in \S\ref{sec:baselines}; the comparison therefore covers published generic methods, reference implementations and standard baselines, and any reviewer asking for an adaptive-NIDS system reproduction is asking for something this design does not provide. Comparisons are between fixed policies; no learned meta-controller was evaluated. Cross-size contrasts in this block are seed-paired only.

\textbf{Scope of the formal, diagnostic and chronological instruments.} The quantum detector, the full sequential frontier and the mild-drift matrix remain evaluated under the historical frozen-transformer policy; VBC-SG was not re-run under size-matched own pipelines, so none of those numbers should be assumed to transfer. VBC-SG's guarantee --- where it applies --- controls false probe-superiority \emph{on the probe distribution}, not future deployment harm; this equals a bound on future harm only when the probe is representative, an assumption our own stale-probe and coverage results show can fail (the empirical-Bernstein sequence exceeds nominal $\alpha$ under strong within-probe autocorrelation), and its deployment-long budget is prohibitive in its maximally-stacked stratified form at $b{=}64$. The gate assumes a small labeled probe at decision time; we report its size ($\approx$32--64 adjudicated flows per confirmed drift) as an incremental adjudication count under the evaluated protocol --- not an estimate of inspected-flow acquisition cost in an operationally imbalanced deployment, which is not modeled; under genuinely random inspection the gate's harm-regime protection dissolves at $\pi = 0.01$, and the initial study's stronger claim, which rested on a probe conditioned to contain one attack, is withdrawn. Label latency is modeled for the probe (up to 20 windows stale) and the candidate batch (5--20 windows; Table~\ref{tab:latency}); asynchronous or partial labeling and candidate-aware label-flipping adversaries are untested. The thirteen chronological replays are external boundary evidence: they provide no estimate or bound on how frequently harm occurs in deployment, most CICIDS streams collapse into deep-benefit territory, ToN-IoT ships no timestamps, so the controlled harm benchmark has no chronological counterpart, and the registered chronological family is structurally easy to satisfy where the incumbent collapses. Quantum kernels are classically simulated, so the reported 114$\times$ monitoring overhead is a simulation figure.

\textbf{Statistical granularity and multiplicity.} Seeds quantify within-regime sampling variability, not new environments: the external units are three datasets and seven regimes, and confidence intervals should be read at that granularity (Supplementary \S S3 reports leave-one-out behaviour). Multiplicity-adjusted $p$-values are computed directly from the paired per-seed contrasts using a deterministic centered paired bootstrap test, with Holm \cite{holm1979simple} controlling the six-test confirmatory family, Holm within each preregistered family of the symmetric replication (Supplementary \S S7), the zero-drift size-matched control (\S S8), the full-drift size control (\S S9) and the common-harness comparison (\S S10), and Benjamini--Hochberg \cite{benjamini1995controlling} applied separately to the pre-declared 15-cell budget-frontier block and the seven-replay chronological block (Supplementary \S S6); frozen-side cells, 512-vs-never columns and anchor-versus-anchor contrasts in the common-harness block are descriptive and uncorrected. \emph{No conclusion depends on the correction}: every nominally significant contrast survives its adjustment, in every family.


\section{Conclusion}


A drift alarm can justify constructing a challenger, but not promoting it. In this study the apparent outcome of promotion changed materially when two comparability asymmetries between challenger and incumbent were corrected: with incumbent-owned frozen preprocessing removed, the mean full-drift harm did not persist; with nominal candidate evidence matched to the incumbent's, the residual zero-drift deficit disappeared, and under real drift the larger candidates improved promotion outcomes by $+0.82$ to $+1.66$ balanced-accuracy points in all three benchmarks. Under those comparable conditions, point and strict validation ceased to provide average benefit, strict validation imposed a measurable cost in one cell, and a registered common-harness comparison found no universal winner among published estimators, reference monitors, standard baselines and the gates --- with the apparent ordering of policies itself depending on the comparability of the candidate generator. Validate the challenger when its construction or evidence conditions remain asymmetric or uncertain; validation is not a substitute for building a comparably constructed, comparably evidenced challenger. Adaptive-system evaluation should therefore establish incumbent--challenger comparability before interpreting promotion harm or comparing promotion policies. The net-harm findings of this paper are configuration-dependent, the registered zero-drift outcome remains ATTENUATION, the chronological replays delimit generalization without bounding deployment harm frequency, and everything reproduces from the public artifact.
